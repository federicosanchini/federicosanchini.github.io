Two correlated geometric Brownian motions

The basket contains one unit of each asset. This extends the supplied pricing notebook with a put option, dividends, reproducible randomness and rate sensitivity. All prices use one consistent, unspecified currency.

Sᵢ(T) = Sᵢ(t) exp[(r − qᵢ − ½σᵢ²)τ + σᵢ√τ Zᵢ]
Z₂ = ρX + √(1 − ρ²)Y   ·   Z₁ = X   ·   τ = T − t

X and Y are independent standard normal draws. For a call the payoff is max(S₁(T) + S₂(T) − K, 0); for a put it is max(K − S₁(T) − S₂(T), 0). The estimated value is the mean payoff discounted by exp(−rτ).

Precision and sensitivities

The approximate 95% interval is price ± 1.96 × standard error of discounted payoffs. It measures simulation error, not model risk. A zero standard error with zero observed payouts does not rule out rare payouts. Increase the sample size for tail events.

Greeks use finite differences with the same Gaussian draws for every bump. Delta and gamma use your spot bump. Vega uses a 0.5 percentage point volatility bump (forward at zero); rho uses a 0.05 percentage point rate bump. Theta rolls remaining maturity down by one day, or the available fraction of a day near expiry, normalized to a daily rate. At expiry theta is unavailable.

Sensitivity charts reuse up to 15,000 draws for responsiveness; the headline price and Greeks use your full sample count. Their estimates may differ slightly. At extreme volatility or maturity, rare lognormal tail events may require many more samples.

Assumptions

European exercise, constant rates, continuous dividend yields, constant volatilities and correlation, and no transaction costs. Real-world paths use μ as the price drift excluding dividends. Inputs are model scenarios, not live market data.
